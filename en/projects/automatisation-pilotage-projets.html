Building a project management tool

I led the redesign of project monitoring at SmartPixel, bringing commercial, operational and accounting data together in a shared environment for financial management.

My role
Leading the redesign and structuring performance indicators
Tools & areas
Jira · Tempo · Coupler.io · QuickBooks · HubSpot

The starting point

Project monitoring relied on a metrics system disconnected from our production environment. Working time was recorded in Tempo, alongside Jira, while commercial information, invoices and expenses were spread across several tools and files.

This fragmentation made it difficult to reconcile what had been sold, what was actually being produced, and what had been invoiced or collected. Understanding a project’s financial position required piecing this information together.

The goal

Build a consolidated view of each project, connecting commercial commitments with operational execution and accounting data.

I wanted a single environment to track hours used, costs, billing and collections, while anticipating remaining work and profitability at completion.

The approach

Connecting sources and structuring data flows

I led the implementation of integration flows connecting production, sales and accounting data. Time entries from Tempo are retrieved through token-authenticated access using Coupler.io data flows, then loaded into a raw-data sheet.

A master file consolidates these records with data from QuickBooks, HubSpot, accounting tracking files and sales calculators. The data is then filtered, matched and aggregated into a project-level management view.

Reconciling sold hours, actual work and remaining effort

The system compares hours sold, including 3D and programming work, with time actually recorded in production. Detailed time entries make it possible to trace contributions by person, project and Jira task, and check timesheet completeness.

Estimating the remaining effort is the main management input entered manually. Combined with actual hours, it provides an estimate of hours at completion and a progress measure based on production effort.

Reconstructing each project’s economics

I structured the indicators to match revenue with labour costs, subcontracting and external purchases allocated to projects.

The same view includes billing milestones, amounts invoiced, upcoming billing stages and payments received. It distinguishes production progress from billing progress and cash collections.

Anticipating future workload

Combining hour volumes with delivery dates also allows production workload to be projected by period. Monitoring becomes a tool for anticipating capacity requirements and informing trade-offs.

Results & next steps

This redesign turned scattered records into consolidated, traceable management information that can be used at project level.

It helps explain differences between sales assumptions and execution, track costs and margins, identify billing stages and project future workload.

My contribution was to connect finance and production needs: define the information required, structure its reconciliation and build a shared understanding of project performance.

From sales to delivery: one shared view of every project.
